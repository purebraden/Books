# CHAPTER 34

Kael slept in pieces.

Not dreams---intervals. Minutes torn loose from one another, stitched
together by pain and the constant awareness that if

he slept too deeply, he might not wake at all.

The storage loft smelled of old grain and canal rot. He'd chosen it
because no one wanted it. No guards. No clerks. No reason for anyone
important to ever step inside unless they were lost or desperate.

Kael woke with his jaw clenched so hard it ached.

Light crept through the warped boards above, pale and gray. Morning
again. Another day the city would redraw itself around him.

He sat up slowly and checked his injuries.

The ankle was swollen but stable. The forearm cut had stopped bleeding.
His ribs screamed when he twisted, but nothing ground or shifted wrong.

He was still functional.

Barely.

Kael wrapped himself tighter, then leaned back against the wall and
listened.

No boots.

No voices.

The hunt had cooled overnight---not because it had failed, but because
it had learned. Gravewater never rushed twice in the same way. It
waited, recalculated, then closed its hand again with more certainty.

Kael closed his eyes and let the knowing settle.

It didn't show him immediate danger.

It showed him convergence.

Three threads kept pulling his attention toward the same place.

The Counting House on Mireline.

Not the public floor---the sealed vault beneath it.

The ledger had hinted at it before, but now the knowing sharpened the
outline: a private reserve where confiscated

assets went to be "held," where bribes cooled into legitimacy, where
corruption laundered itself until it smelled like law.

Gold.

Documents.

Seals.

Enough money to buy a ship and the silence around it.

Enough damage to force the city to react loudly instead of quietly.

Kael opened his eyes.

This wasn't just theft.

This was an amputation.

If he took it, Gravewater would bleed openly.

And it would never forgive him.

Kael stayed in the loft until dusk, listening to the city reassemble
itself. He heard rumors carried by the water---voices drifting from
boats and walkways.

"They say he hurt three Watchmen."

"They say he vanished through a wall."

"They say he's already dead."

"No---he's still here. You can feel it."

Kael flinched at that.

He didn't like being felt.

When night came, he moved.

Slow. Careful. Not through his old routes, not through the alleys that
remembered him. He crossed the city like a ghost with amnesia, relying
on instinct and the knowing rather than memory.

The Counting House rose from Mireline like a judgment---clean stone,
iron gates, windows too narrow to be useful. Guards stood openly now.
Not many. Enough.

They weren't hiding anything anymore.

That told Kael everything he needed to know.

He watched from across the street, crouched in the shadow of a shuttered
spice shop. The front doors closed at midnight. Clerks left in pairs.
Guards changed shifts with clockwork precision.

The city loved routine.

Kael waited until the last clerk vanished down the street, then crossed
quietly.

He didn't approach the front.

He slipped into a side courtyard where rainwater pooled and moss crept
along the stone. A single guard leaned against the wall there, bored,
half-asleep.

Kael watched him for a full minute.

Then he moved.

Five feet sideways---just enough to be behind the man---and pressed his
forearm against the guard's throat, cutting off air. The man struggled
for seconds, then went limp.

Kael lowered him gently and dragged him into shadow.

No blood.

No noise.

Inside, the Counting House was all echoes and stone. Kael moved through
corridors that smelled of ink and coin, down stairs that led beneath the
street. The vault door waited at the bottom, iron thick enough to
discourage hope.

Kael knelt and listened.

No guards inside.

Of course not.

The city trusted locks more than people.

Kael touched the vault door.

The knowing flared---not warning, but instruction. Pressure points. Weak
seams. Where weight had settled over decades.

He worked slowly, carefully, fingers numb with cold and pain.

The door opened with a soft groan that sounded too loud in the silence.

Inside, gold waited.

Not piles. Not chests.

Stacks. Ledgers. Sealed boxes. A system.

Kael's breath caught.

This wasn't one theft.

This was the city's spine.

He took what he could carry---coin, sealed papers, a small lockbox
marked with a sigil he didn't recognize but felt wrong to leave behind.

Then the knowing screamed.

Footsteps above.

Many.

Not guards on patrol.

This was response.

Kael swore under his breath, slung the pack over his shoulder, and ran.

He didn't bother with stealth now.

He burst up the stairs, slammed into a guard, and vanished sideways
through a wall into the courtyard. Shouts erupted behind him.

Lanterns flared.

A horn sounded.

The city roared awake.

Kael ran.

Not away from the Counting House.

Through the city.

He wanted them to see this.

He wanted them to know.

By the time he vanished into the warren of streets near the

canal, Gravewater was screaming his presence from tower to tower.

He didn't go back to the brothel.

He didn't go back to any safe place.

He kept moving until his lungs burned and his ankle threatened to give
out entirely.

Only when he reached the water's edge did he stop, crouching in shadow
as bells rang across the city behind him.

Kael looked down at the pack.

Heavy.

Worth everything.

And now impossible to hide.

Gravewater would not absorb this quietly.

By morning, there would be warrants.

By noon, there would be names.

By nightfall, there would be nowhere left to stand.

Kael closed his eyes and let the city's fury wash over him.

This was it.

The theft that burned the bridges.

The choice that made escape the only remaining move.

Tomorrow, he would run for the sea.

Tonight, he vanished one last time into the city that would never
forgive him.
