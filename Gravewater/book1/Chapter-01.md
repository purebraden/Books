# CHAPTER 1

The job was small enough to be honest about.

That alone made Kael uneasy.

They met before dawn on a narrow platform that hadn't bothered
pretending it was level in years. The water below moved in slow, uneven
bands, slapping gently against the

pylons with the sound of something patient testing a boundary. Tessa
stood near the edge, boots planted wide, watching reflections rather
than the street. Bram leaned against a support beam, hood up, hands
still. Mirek arrived last, loud as always, carrying a sack that clinked
once before he caught it and stopped.

"Relax," Mirek said, grinning. "That was an accident."

Tessa didn't look at him. "Accidents make noise."

"So do you," Mirek replied cheerfully.

Kael watched them with half his attention and the route with the rest.
The lift platform two streets over had cycled twice already---too often
for this hour---which meant someone important was moving, or someone
wanted to look important. Either way, it shortened their window.

"Five minutes," Kael said. "We miss one patrol and catch the next."

Mirek's grin softened into something more professional. "Then we don't
miss."

The target was a storage annex built into the side of a counting house
that no longer counted much of anything. Its original owners had fled
upward years ago, leaving behind records no one trusted and goods no one
wanted to claim. The Watch still made appearances, but out of habit more
than interest.

Bram went first, moving ahead without being told. He checked the latch
with two fingers, paused, then nodded once and stepped aside. Kael
followed, testing the board beneath his foot before committing weight.
It held. Barely.

Inside, the air smelled like old rope and damp paper. The annex had been
divided and redivided over the years, walls added where they were needed
and left when they weren't.

Tessa slipped past Kael and took point, her knife already out but low,
more a reminder than a threat.

Mirek waited until they were all inside before closing the door behind
them. He did it gently. Kael noticed. Mirek noticed that Kael noticed
and winked.

They moved quickly but without hurry. Tessa counted steps under her
breath, stopping just before the third partition. She tapped twice on
the wall, listening. Nothing hollow. She shifted left, tried again, then
nodded Kael forward.

"Your turn," she murmured.

Kael knelt, feeling the stone with his palm. There---slight warmth where
it shouldn't be. A recent repair. He slid his fingers along the seam
until he found the catch hidden behind a smear of pitch. It gave with a
soft click.

Mirek exhaled quietly. "Every time."

"Every time," Tessa agreed.

They took only what had been agreed upon: sealed tins of lamp oil, two
coils of wire, and a wrapped packet of tools that hadn't seen daylight
in years. No ledgers. No loose goods. No souvenirs.

Kael kept count automatically, not just of items but of time. The
pressure behind his eyes stayed low, a dull awareness rather than a
warning. That was good. Jobs that felt wrong announced themselves early.

On the way out, Mirek caught his sleeve.

"You hear that?" Mirek whispered.

Kael listened. Footsteps. Two sets. Too light for Watch boots, too
regular for dockhands.

Tessa didn't wait. She shifted their exit without comment, guiding them
through a narrow service corridor that sloped downward and then up
again, skirting a channel that smelled faintly of rust and salt. Bram
took the rear, pausing once to

adjust a loose plank so it wouldn't complain later.

They emerged two levels over and one down, the city rearranging itself
around them as if nothing had happened.

Only when they were clear did Mirek speak. "See? Easy."

Tessa finally looked at him. "You nearly tripped the wire."

"Nearly," Mirek said. "Didn't."

Kael handed Mirek one of the tins. "You rushed."

Mirek blinked, then shrugged. "Felt like we had time."

Kael met his eyes. "We didn't."

Mirek considered that, then nodded. "All right."

That was as close as Mirek ever came to apology.

They regrouped in a dry alcove near the old fish stairs, where someone
had once painted a sign promising fresh catch daily. The paint had
peeled away long ago, leaving only the word fresh ghosted on the stone.

Tessa broke open a small packet and passed it around. Flatbread,
slightly stale but serviceable. Kael took a piece and ate slowly,
listening to the city resume its normal sounds.

Mirek launched into a story about a job years ago that had gone nowhere
and meant nothing. Bram listened without comment, eyes tracking movement
along the water. Tessa checked the wire coil twice, then once more for
good measure.

Kael watched them all and felt the quiet satisfaction of something done
properly. No excess. No drama. Just work.

For a moment---only a moment---the city felt manageable.

They split up after, as they always did, melting back into Gravewater's
shifting routes. Kael headed upward, following a path that would take
him past the lift and then around it, avoiding attention without
pretending to.

As he walked, he noticed a man arguing with a Watch patrol

over a crate that had shifted during the night. The Watchman's voice was
calm, practiced. The man's wasn't. Kael didn't stop, but he listened
long enough to know how it would end.

The pressure behind his eyes stirred faintly, then settled.

He reached the stairs and climbed, the sound of water fading as stone
took over. Above him, Gravewater adjusted again, always finding new ways
to hold.

Kael moved with it, as he always had, unaware of how fragile that
balance truly was.
