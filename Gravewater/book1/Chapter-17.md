# CHAPTER 17

Kael didn't feel free.

He felt loose---as if the world had stopped holding him in place and now
expected him to fall where gravity decided.

He staggered through the maintenance passage, shoulder scraping stone,
boots catching on uneven boards. Every

breath hurt. His muscles shook with aftershock, not pain exactly but the
memory of strain refusing to let go. When he tried to straighten, his
vision swam and narrowed, dark at the edges.

He kept moving anyway.

Stopping meant being found.

The passage bent upward, gradually at first, then more sharply. The
smell changed---less iron, more rot and old water. Kael dragged himself
up a short ladder, hands slipping on the rungs, and collapsed briefly at
the top, chest heaving.

Voices echoed below.

Not close. Not yet.

Kael forced himself up and limped forward, emerging into a low-ceilinged
service corridor he recognized. He'd crawled through it once as a kid,
chasing a dropped coin. It had felt like a secret then.

Now it felt like a throat.

He moved on instinct, turning where memory told him to turn, avoiding
spots that would creak too loudly. His ankle screamed with every step.
He ignored it. Pain was manageable. Capture wasn't.

As he climbed, the city came back to life around him.

Not all at once---just hints at first. Distant footsteps. A door opening
and closing above. The murmur of voices that belonged to people who
lived here, not people who maintained what they tried not to think
about.

Kael reached a narrow access hatch and pushed it open with his shoulder.

He spilled out into a storage alcove beneath a fishmonger's platform,
the stink of old brine and spoiled scales hitting him like a slap. He
gagged, clamping a hand over his mouth until the nausea passed.

Above him, someone laughed.

Normal laughter.

The sound almost broke him.

Kael waited until his breathing steadied, then eased himself upright and
slid into the open, pulling his hood low. His movements were clumsy now,
none of the smooth confidence he'd once relied on. He felt eyes on him
immediately---not Watch, not yet, but people noticing a limping boy with
blood on his sleeve.

Visibility was dangerous.

Kael angled toward the crowd.

The knowing stirred weakly, like something waking from a hard blow. He
felt it pressing at the edges of his perception, eager to reassert
itself, to tell him where to go, who to avoid.

"No," he muttered under his breath.

He pushed it down, focusing on sound instead---footsteps, water, voices
overlapping. He let the noise blur into cover and moved with it, another
body flowing through Gravewater's narrow veins.

He made it three platforms before the first sign that the city was
responding.

A Watch whistle cut through the air.

Not close. But not distant either.

Kael's heart slammed. He ducked under a hanging net and changed
direction abruptly, forcing his legs to move faster than they wanted to.
Pain flared white-hot in his ankle. He nearly went down, caught himself
on a piling, and kept going.

"Hey!" someone shouted behind him.

Kael didn't look back.

He slipped down a short stair, vaulted a low railing, and vanished into
a side passage just as boots thundered onto

the platform above. Shouts followed---confused, overlapping.

Orders being formed on the fly.

Good, Kael thought dimly. Confusion bought time.

He pushed through a hanging tarp and nearly collided with a woman
carrying a crate. She yelped, dropping it. Fish spilled across the
boards.

"Sorry," Kael gasped automatically, already moving past.

The woman stared after him, mouth open.

Kael didn't wait to see if she called out.

He cut left, then right, then ducked through a narrow gap between
buildings that opened onto a damp stairwell spiraling upward. His legs
burned. His lungs felt too small.

Halfway up, the whistle sounded again---closer this time.

Kael swore and forced himself faster.

He burst out onto a mid-tier walkway just as a Watch patrol turned onto
it from the far end. Three men. Lanterns low. Faces sharp with purpose.

Kael froze for half a heartbeat.

Then he bolted.

Shouts erupted instantly. Someone pointed. Boots pounded.

Kael ran on raw will, ignoring the way his ankle threatened to fold with
every step. He shoved through startled pedestrians, took a hard turn
that scraped skin from his shoulder, and dove down a sloping ramp that
ended in a tangle of old mooring lines.

He crashed through them and rolled hard, the impact knocking the air
from his lungs.

He lay there gasping, stars bursting behind his eyes, listening to the
chaos above---shouts, confused orders, someone yelling that they'd lost
him.

Kael didn't move.

He counted breaths. One. Two. Three.

The knowing pressed faintly, trying to show him which way to go next. He
ignored it, forcing his body to lead instead of his mind.

When the noise above shifted away---footsteps retreating, whistles
moving in the wrong direction---Kael dragged himself to his feet and
limped deeper into the tangle, keeping low.

He didn't stop until his legs finally gave out in a narrow alcove that
smelled of wet rope and old oil.

Kael slid down the wall and sat there, shaking.

He pressed his forehead to his knees and breathed, slow and deliberate,
until the world stopped spinning.

He was out.

Not safe. Not hidden.

But no longer held.

The city would adjust. Reports would move. Names would be passed from
mouth to mouth, imperfectly at first, then with growing certainty.

Kael wiped his face on his sleeve and looked out at the water through a
gap in the boards. It lapped softly against the pylons, innocent and
patient.

Somewhere above, Gravewater was waking fully to the fact that he had
slipped its grasp.

And it would not be as slow next time.

Kael pushed himself up, testing his weight carefully, and started moving
again---away from the lower tiers, away from the places that had tried
to turn him into something useful.

Toward whatever came next.
