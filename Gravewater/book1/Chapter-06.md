# CHAPTER 6

They didn't sleep.

They rested in shifts, backs to damp stone, listening to Gravewater
breathe around them. The city never truly went quiet, but there were
hours when its noises softened into something almost gentle---water
slipping instead of slapping, boards creaking instead of cracking. It
was during one of those hours that Tessa spoke.

"Next time," she said quietly, "we don't go down."

Kael sat beside her, knees drawn up, fingers worrying at a loose thread
on his sleeve. "There won't be a next time like that."

Tessa huffed a humorless laugh. "That's what people say right before
there is."

They were alone on the platform. Bram had gone to scout, moving with the
careful distance of someone who needed motion to keep from thinking.
Kael watched the dark water beyond the pylons, forcing himself not to
look too long.

"You all right?" Tessa asked.

He nodded automatically, then stopped. "No."

She didn't press him. That was one of the things he trusted about
her---she knew when questions were a form of violence.

"We'll get through it," she said instead.

Kael glanced at her. "You don't sound convinced."

Tessa leaned back against the railing, eyes fixed on nothing. "I sound
realistic."

For a moment, the weight of everything pressed in on him---the ledger
hidden too close for comfort, the Thing Below pressing upward through
water and iron, Mirek's absence like a missing note in a familiar
rhythm. He wanted to say something meaningful. Something that might
change the shape of what was coming.

He didn't.

Tessa nudged his shoulder with hers. "Hey. Whatever happens, you kept us
alive longer than we deserved."

Kael swallowed. "That's not---"

She shook her head. "It is."

Footsteps echoed on the boards behind them.

Kael turned too late.

The attack came without warning, without drama. A blur of movement, a
sharp sound like something tearing, and Tessa was gone from beside him,
pulled forward with brutal force. She hit the railing hard, breath
knocked from her in a sound Kael would hear in his dreams for years to
come.

"Tessa!" he shouted.

She twisted, knife flashing once as she fought to regain footing. Kael
caught a glimpse of her face---focused, furious, unafraid. Then
something struck her from the side, fast and precise.

She fell.

The water below surged, rising unnaturally high, swallowing sound and
light alike. Kael lunged, fingers scraping wood, missing her by inches
as she disappeared into the dark.

"No---!"

He leaned over the railing, heart pounding, eyes straining. The water
churned once, violently, then stilled.

Tessa did not resurface.

The city did not pause.

Bram appeared at Kael's side, grabbing him before he could throw himself
over the edge. "Kael. Stop."

"She's---" Kael choked. "She's still---"

The words died in his throat as the truth settled in his chest, heavy
and absolute.

Tessa was gone.

Kael sagged against the railing, every sound suddenly too loud, too
sharp. The world narrowed to the ache in his chest and the hollow where
something vital had been ripped away.

Around them, Gravewater continued its routines. A lantern flickered to
life on a distant platform. Someone laughed. Water slapped stone with
indifferent rhythm.

Bram swore, a raw sound torn from his chest. "We need to move."

Kael didn't hear him. He was staring at the water, at the place where
Tessa had vanished, replaying the moment again and again as if
repetition might undo it.

This was his fault.

If he had chosen differently.

If he had listened to her.

If he had acted faster.

The pressure behind his eyes surged, no longer a dull ache but a searing
weight that drove him to his knees. He gasped, clutching his head as the
world fractured around him.

The knowing came like a flood.

Not gently. Not cleanly.

Truth slammed into him from every direction at once---lies peeling away,
debts glowing with terrible clarity. He saw the hidden lines of
obligation binding Gravewater together, the costs quietly paid and those
left unpaid. He saw the ledger's weight ripple outward, connecting to
the Thing Below, to the iron bands straining beneath the city.

He saw Tessa's death not as an accident, but as a consequence.

The knowing hurt.

It burned behind his eyes, down his spine, through his chest, leaving
him shaking and retching on the boards. He tried to shut it out, to curl
inward and make it stop, but it pressed on regardless, uncaring of his
wishes.

Bram's voice reached him distantly. "Kael? Kael!"

Kael lifted his head, eyes unfocused. He could see Bram's fear now---not
just the surface emotion, but the weight beneath it, the unspoken
calculation of survival. He could see

the city's attention turning toward them, invisible threads tightening.

"Don't," Kael whispered.

Bram froze. "Don't what?"

"Lie," Kael said, and hated himself for it.

The knowing receded slightly, enough for him to breathe, but it didn't
leave. It settled instead, heavy and watchful, a presence he knew would
never fully let go.

Tessa was dead.

Understanding hadn't saved her. Power hadn't arrived in time. It had
come only after the loss, as if to ensure Kael would never be able to
look away again.

Bram backed away slowly. "What's wrong with you?"

Kael dragged himself to his feet, legs trembling. He looked once more at
the water, at the place where Tessa had vanished, and felt the full
weight of what he now knew.

The city had taken her.

And it expected to be paid.
