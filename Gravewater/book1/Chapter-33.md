# CHAPTER 33

Kael didn't move until the light shifted.

He counted breaths instead---slow, shallow ones that kept the pain from
sharpening. The ankle throbbed like a second heart. Every pulse reminded
him that this wasn't a clean escape. It was a survival margin measured
in inches and seconds.

Voices passed again, closer this time.

"...split the line---no, not there---"

A pause.

"...check the rubble."

Kael's jaw tightened.

They were adapting. Not searching randomly---narrowing. The knowing
traced their angles without flaring, drawing quiet vectors through the
dark. If they came into the collapsed building, they'd do it carefully.
Too carefully. That meant clerks nearby. Witnesses. Paper.

Kael waited until the boots shifted away, then slid backward deeper into
the wreckage. A beam creaked under his weight. He froze, heart slamming,
then eased pressure off it one muscle at a time.

The city listened for mistakes.

He refused to give it one.

He found a crawlspace between fallen timbers and wriggled into it, grime
coating his clothes, splinters biting his palms. The smell was rot and
old rain. Perfect. He vanished five feet

forward, reappearing where the darkness was thickest, then stayed still.

Minutes passed.

The voices thinned.

Then silence.

Not relief. Not yet.

Silence meant they'd decided he wasn't there right now---and that they'd
repositioned.

Kael crawled out the far side and limped into a service lane he didn't
like. Too straight. Too visible from above. He moved anyway, keeping to
the wall, hood up, head down.

A door ahead opened.

Kael stopped.

A woman stepped out carrying a basket, glanced at him, then froze---not
in fear, but recognition. Her eyes flicked to his ankle, to the blood at
his sleeve.

Kael shook his head once.

Please.

She hesitated---just long enough for the city to notice
hesitation---then turned away sharply and shut the door.

Kael exhaled.

That counted as a mercy.

It also counted as a data point.

He moved again, slower now. He needed distance more than speed. He cut
left into a tangle of alleys where the ground sloped unevenly and water
pooled in places that made boots loud. The knowing sketched risk
ahead---two Watchmen on a cross street, a clerk just out of sight, a
pair of men pretending to argue over crates.

Kael changed direction twice, slipped through a half-open gate, and
nearly collided with a Watch patrol coming the

other way.

Too close.

He felt the moment stretch---the Watchman's eyes widening, his mouth
opening to shout, the baton rising---

Kael slipped sideways, appeared behind the second Watchman, and shoved
him hard into the first. Both stumbled. The shout turned into a curse.

Kael ran.

A whistle screamed.

Another answered it.

Now it was loud.

Now it was public.

Now there was no pretending this was still quiet containment.

Kael vaulted a low wall, landed badly, and bit back a cry as pain flared
hot and bright. He kept moving anyway, lungs burning, vision narrowing.
He ducked under a line of hanging nets and slid into a fish market
stall, overturning a basket of ice and scales as he went.

A hand grabbed for him and missed.

"After him!"

He slipped again---five feet---appearing behind a stack of crates and
smashing his elbow into a man's ribs hard enough to steal breath. He
didn't stay to finish it. He couldn't afford to.

He broke into a run toward a narrow stair that led up to a tenement
roof.

Halfway up, something slammed into his side.

He hit the wall hard, stars bursting across his vision. He tasted blood.

A Watchman had tackled him from the side, baton clattering away.

Kael reacted on instinct.

Knife out. Low. Fast.

The blade cut shallow across the man's thigh---enough to cripple, not
kill. The Watchman screamed and fell back, clutching his leg.

Kael staggered up the rest of the stairs, hauled himself onto the roof,
and collapsed behind a chimney, chest heaving.

Below, voices surged. Orders. Shouting. The city boiling over.

Kael pressed his forehead to the cool brick and forced himself to
breathe.

He was bleeding now---forearm, ankle, somewhere near his ribs. Not
badly. But enough.

Enough that he couldn't keep doing this.

He peered over the edge.

The Watch had changed formation already. Lanterns repositioned. Men
fanned out wider, covering exits he hadn't even considered. Clerks
pointed and spoke quickly, hands moving over paper as if writing in the
air.

They weren't improvising.

They were updating doctrine.

Kael felt something cold settle in his gut.

This wasn't about one theft anymore. Or one body. Or even the ledger.

This was about proving that awakened problems could be contained.

He stayed on the roof until the noise thinned, then moved---slow,
careful, favoring the ankle. He crossed rooftops he'd never used before,
dropped into a tenement stairwell, and vanished into the interior like a
rumor retreating into walls.

By the time he reached a half-forgotten storage loft near the canal, he
was shaking.

He barricaded the door with a crate, slid down the wall, and

sat in the dark with his head between his knees.

His hands trembled.

Not fear.

Aftershock.

He pressed his fingers to his ribs and winced. Nothing broken. Just
bruised. He tied off the bleeding and leaned back, staring at the
ceiling beams.

The knowing settled, quiet and merciless.

Daylight was no longer an option.

The brothel was no longer safe.

Neutral ground didn't exist.

The city had committed.

And if he stayed, it would escalate again---more men, tighter nets,
fewer exits---until the choice was no longer his.

Kael closed his eyes.

He didn't think about the sea.

Not yet.

He thought about one last theft. One last move big enough to buy
distance. Money enough to disappear. Chaos enough to break pursuit.

After that...

After that, he would run.

Not because he'd lost.

But because Gravewater had decided to prove a point.

And Kael was done letting the city write his ending.
