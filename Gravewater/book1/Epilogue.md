# EPILOGUE

They found him after the ship screamed.

Kael had been hidden well---wedged deep between crates wrapped in
oilcloth, body folded into a space that smelled of pitch and old iron.
The freighter's crew hadn't known he was there. They wouldn't have cared
if they had. Stowaways were common enough. Dead stowaways even more so.

The sea didn't care either.

The first impact felt like the world being struck with a hammer.

Wood shrieked. Iron rang. The freighter lurched so hard Kael slammed
shoulder-first into the hull, teeth clacking as pain flared bright and
immediate. Shouts erupted above him---confused, panicked, then terrified
as the second impact came, heavier than the first.

Not a collision.

An attack.

Kael pressed himself flat against the hull, heart pounding, the knowing
stirring in sharp, unwelcome fragments. Above him, boots thundered.
Someone screamed. Something tore through wood with a sound like cloth
ripping, only deeper, wetter.

A grappling hook bit into the deck.

Then another.

The ship slowed abruptly, dragged sideways by forces that

did not care about wind or current. Lantern light spilled down through
gaps in the boards as men shouted orders that no one followed correctly.

Pirates.

Not the dockside kind. Not thieves with boats and bad ideas.

These moved wrong.

Kael felt it before he saw it---the way the air thickened, the way sound
bent around motion that shouldn't have been possible on a rocking deck.
The knowing flared hard enough to make his vision blur.

Awakened.

Several of them.

Stronger than him.

Faster.

The first pirate dropped into the hold like gravity had been optional.
He landed without bracing, boots hitting the deck with a dull thud that
sent a shiver through the beams. Lantern light caught his face for a
heartbeat---scarred, calm, eyes too focused for the chaos above.

He smiled.

"Well," the pirate said pleasantly, "this one's got teeth."

More shapes followed---three, four---descending ropes or simply stepping
down where ladders should have been. One of them laughed softly as he
landed, rolling his shoulders like a man stretching before work.

The crew never made it to the hold.

Kael heard the fighting above---brief, vicious, one-sided. Steel rang.
Someone shouted a command that was cut off mid-word. Something heavy hit
the deck and didn't get back up.

Kael stayed still.

He wrapped himself in shadow and silence, pressing his back to the hull
so hard it hurt. His heart hammered against

his ribs, loud in his own ears, but the pirates didn't seem rushed.

They knew.

One of them crouched and ran a hand along the crates, fingers brushing
wood, oilcloth, rope. He stopped inches from Kael's hiding place.

"There," he said.

Kael moved.

He slipped sideways---five feet---reappearing behind the pirate and
driving his knife toward the base of the man's skull with everything he
had.

The blade stopped.

Two fingers closed around Kael's wrist, impossibly fast, grip like iron.
The pirate turned his head slightly, looking almost curious.

"Cute," he said.

Kael tried to vanish again.

Nothing happened.

The knowing screamed---pressure, presence, something anchored in the
space itself. Another pirate laughed outright.

"Oh," she said, voice bright. "He thought he could do that."

The first pirate twisted Kael's wrist. Pain exploded up his arm and the
knife clattered to the deck. Kael gasped, trying to pull free, but the
man barely seemed to notice the resistance.

"You're awakened," the pirate said. "Small one. Early. Still soft."

He shoved Kael backward. Kael hit the crates hard and slid down, breath
punched out of him. Before he could move, a boot pressed against his
chest, pinning him in place.

Kael looked up.

There were five of them in the hold now.

All calm.

All watching him.

All wrong in subtle ways---standing too steady on a rocking ship, eyes
too sharp, movements too precise. The knowing flared again, showing him
glimpses of strength layered on strength, of powers honed through use
and blood.

They were not hunters.

They were veterans.

One of them crouched and tilted Kael's chin up with the tip of a blade.
"You're not crew."

Kael swallowed. "I didn't hurt anyone."

The pirate snorted. "Neither did the sea. That never stops it."

Another pirate leaned against a barrel, arms crossed. "What's a
city-shadow doing stowing away on junk like this?"

Kael's heart raced. He didn't answer.

The first pirate studied him for a long moment, then smiled again---not
cruelly. Assessing. Interested.

"Gravewater," he said. "I thought I smelled ink."

Kael's blood went cold.

The pirate straightened and looked at the others. "We've got ourselves a
problem," he said. "Or an opportunity."

He looked back down at Kael.

"Depends what he's worth."

The ship groaned beneath them as it was pulled fully off course, sails
snapping as they were cut loose. Outside, the sea waited---dark,
patient, full of things that did not care who Kael had been in the city
he'd fled.

Kael lay pinned to the deck, bruised, bleeding, staring up at pirates
who could crush him without effort.

Gravewater was already gone behind him.

And whatever came next would not be kinder.
