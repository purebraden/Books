# CHAPTER 7

Kael woke to the sound of dripping water and the taste of metal.

For a moment he didn't know where he was. The ceiling above him was low,
wood dark with damp and age. The air smelled of brine and old wax. His
stomach rolled as if the room itself were moving, though he knew it was
only his body trying to recalibrate after the night before.

Then memory arrived.

Tessa's hand on his shoulder. Her voice. The sudden tearing movement.
The water swallowing her like she'd never existed.

Kael sat up too fast and immediately regretted it. Pain flared

behind his eyes, sharp and bright, splitting his skull with a pulse that
felt like light. He gagged, pressing the heel of his hand to his mouth
until the nausea passed.

Across the room, Bram sat against the wall with his knees up, arms
folded loosely. He looked like he hadn't slept at all.

"You're awake," Bram said.

Kael tried to answer and found his throat too dry. He swallowed, then
forced out, "How long?"

"Long enough," Bram replied. His gaze stayed fixed on Kael's face, not
in concern but in assessment.

Kael pushed himself to his feet slowly. The room steadied in a way that
made his skin crawl---too quick, too clean, like his body had decided
balance was optional now.

He caught Bram's expression and understood.

Bram had noticed too.

Kael looked down at his hands. They weren't shaking. They should have
been. They weren't even trembling.

Something in him had settled into a new shape.

"Don't do that," Bram said quietly.

Kael blinked. "Do what?"

Bram's jaw tightened. "Whatever you're doing with your eyes. Like
you're... listening."

Kael started to protest, then felt it---the pressure behind his eyes,
constant now, like a tide held just below the surface. He wasn't
reaching for it deliberately, but it was there, ready, eager.

He turned his head away. "I'm not trying."

"That's worse," Bram said.

Kael swallowed. The words sat in his chest like stones.

He stepped toward the small window and looked out. Gray light filtered
through warped glass, casting the world in dull

bands. Below, Gravewater moved as it always had. People walked, worked,
argued. A vendor shouted. A child ran with something tucked under his
arm.

Kael felt the knowing stir.

It wanted to open. It wanted to show him the hidden accounting beneath
every movement, every gesture, every lie.

He gripped the window frame hard enough to make his fingers ache.

"No," he whispered, not sure who he was addressing.

Bram watched him without speaking.

For a few minutes, Kael did nothing but breathe and count the sensations
that still belonged to him: the sting in his fingers, the damp chill on
his skin, the faint ache in his stomach. It helped, a little, like
anchoring a rope in loose soil.

When he turned back, Bram was still staring.

"You're going to get us killed," Bram said.

Kael felt the pain behind his eyes flare again, as if the knowing
agreed.

"I don't want this," Kael said.

Bram's laugh was short and brittle. "No one wants what they become. That
doesn't stop it."

Kael's mouth tightened. He looked at Bram---really looked---and the
knowing surged despite his resistance. For a heartbeat, Bram's face
became a map of unspoken things: fear, calculation, grief held down so
hard it had become something like anger.

Kael recoiled as if struck.

Bram stood abruptly, hand going to his belt. "Don't."

Kael raised his hands, palms out. "I didn't--- I didn't mean to."

But the knowing didn't care what he meant.

It pressed itself into him anyway, spilling information he didn't want:
Bram's plans forming already, the route he'd

chosen in his head, the decision he hadn't yet spoken.

Kael's stomach lurched. "You're leaving."

Bram's eyes narrowed. "How---"

Kael shook his head violently, as if he could dislodge the knowledge. "I
can see it."

Silence filled the room.

Bram's voice was flat when he spoke. "Then you can see why I have to."

Kael stared at him, breath shallow. "Because I'm dangerous."

"Because you're unpredictable," Bram corrected. "Because whatever
happened to you last night---whatever you are now---people will notice."

Kael's chest tightened. He tried to speak and found the words too large.

"You think I killed her," he said quietly.

Bram's face flickered. Not guilt. Not accusation. Something colder.
"No," he said. "I think the city killed her. And I think you're going to
make it worse by trying to balance it."

Kael flinched.

Bram continued, voice low but steady. "You were always the careful one.
Always counting, always watching. That kept us alive. But now..." He
gestured vaguely toward Kael's face, his eyes. "Now you're not just
watching. You're seeing too much."

Kael felt the knowing pulse again, hungry for more. He forced his gaze
downward.

"What am I supposed to do?" Kael asked, and hated how small his voice
sounded.

Bram's expression didn't soften. "Survive."

Kael laughed once, bitter. "Alone?"

Bram didn't answer immediately. He looked away, jaw tight, as if the
words cost him more than he wanted to admit.

"I'll draw attention off you," Bram said finally. "If I can."

Kael's head lifted. "Why?"

Bram's eyes met his for a moment, and in that moment Kael saw something
human---something like loyalty, buried under fear.

"Because I knew you before this," Bram said. "And I don't want to owe
you for staying."

The sentence landed hard. Bram was doing what Bram always did: choosing
the path that left the least debt behind.

Kael nodded slowly. "Don't get killed."

Bram's mouth twitched, not quite a smile. "Same to you."

He moved to the door, pausing with his hand on the latch. "One more
thing."

Kael waited.

Bram didn't turn. "Don't use it unless you have to."

Kael swallowed. "I'm trying."

"That's not the same as succeeding," Bram said, and then he was gone.

The door clicked shut behind him.

Kael stood alone in the dim room, listening to footsteps fade down the
stairs. The silence that followed was different from before---no longer
shared, no longer temporary. It felt permanent, like a wall closing.

He sank back onto the floor, head in his hands.

The knowing pressed against him like a tide against a barrier. He could
feel it waiting for him to give in, to open his eyes and let it flood
the world with unwanted clarity.

Kael stared at his palms, at the faint grime embedded in the lines of
his skin.

He thought of Tessa---of her voice saying whatever happens. He thought
of Mirek's laugh. He thought of Bram walking

away because staying would turn him into a liability or a corpse.

Kael breathed in and tasted the city: damp wood, salt, old wax, iron.

He understood, with a clarity that didn't require the knowing at all:

He was alone now.

And the cost of seeing too much was that no one could afford to stand
beside him.
