# CHAPTER 8

Kael learned quickly which places were no longer his.

The small alcove near the fish stairs where Mirek used to wait was
occupied now by a pair of dockhands who looked at Kael with open
suspicion. The dry stairwell that smelled of old oil had been sealed
with a new plank, hammered in crookedly, as if the work had been rushed.
Even the narrow bridge over the sluggish channel felt wrong beneath his
feet, the boards flexing too much, too eagerly, as though testing his
weight.

Gravewater had shifted its tolerances.

Kael moved anyway, because stopping meant being seen.

He kept his head down and his eyes unfocused, refusing the knowing with
a stubbornness that bordered on desperation. It pressed against him
constantly now, not like a voice but like pressure in a sealed room,
insisting on release. Every person he passed carried a blur of
impressions just beneath the surface---fears, intentions, unpaid debts
tugging at invisible lines. If he let his attention linger, even for a
heartbeat, the knowing surged, eager to complete the picture.

He didn't let it.

By midmorning the pain behind his eyes had settled into a steady throb.
Not sharp enough to stop him moving. Not dull enough to ignore. His
stomach churned with a hollow nausea that food wouldn't have fixed even
if he'd had any.

He reached one of the higher platforms by habit and stopped short.

This had been Tessa's route. A place where she used to pause and scan
the water, checking angles, counting people without appearing to. Kael
stood there longer than was wise, staring at the empty space beside him
where she should have been.

The absence felt louder than any sound.

He turned away sharply and nearly collided with a woman hauling a crate.
She flinched back, eyes wide, and Kael felt the knowing lurch toward
her, eager.

He shut his eyes and staggered, gripping the railing until the wave
passed.

"I'm sorry," he muttered, though he wasn't sure for what.

The woman didn't answer. She hurried past, glancing over her shoulder
once before disappearing into the crowd.

Kael's hands shook now. He tucked them into his sleeves, clenching his
fists until his nails bit into his palms. Pain helped. It anchored him
in his body, kept his attention from slipping outward.

He moved on.

The city was crowded at this hour, and that made everything worse. Too
many people meant too many impressions pressing at the edges of his
awareness, like voices murmuring just out of reach. He could feel lies
forming before they were spoken, see obligations stretching taut between
strangers who pretended not to know each other.

Kael ducked into a narrow passage to catch his breath.

He slid down the wall and sat, head bowed, breathing through his nose in
short, controlled bursts. Don't look. Don't listen. Don't reach.

For a few blessed moments, the knowing receded, sulking.

Then a shout echoed from the street outside.

Kael froze.

Another shout followed, sharper this time, and the sound of something
breaking. Kael's pulse quickened despite himself. He edged toward the
mouth of the passage and peered out.

A boy---no more than twelve---was running, clutching something to his
chest. A Watchman followed close behind, boots pounding, lantern
swinging wildly. The crowd parted instinctively, faces turning away.

Kael felt the knowing surge, hot and insistent.

Don't, he told himself.

The boy stumbled. The Watchman closed the distance.

Kael swore under his breath.

He opened himself to the knowing.

The world fractured.

Information slammed into him with brutal clarity. The boy wasn't a
thief---not really. He'd taken back something that had been taken from
him first, a small token pawned by a man who owed too many debts to too
many people. Kael saw the imbalance instantly, the way the cost would
ripple outward if the Watchman caught him. Beating. Detainment. A debt
added where one already pressed too hard.

Kael gasped as pain tore through his head, white-hot and blinding. He
doubled over, hands clawing at the stone as nausea surged up his throat.

The knowing didn't care.

It showed him exactly where the Watchman would step next,

exactly when the boy would fall again.

Kael lurched forward.

"Hey!" he shouted, voice cracking.

The Watchman's head snapped up. Kael stepped into the street, heart
hammering, vision swimming. He forced his gaze up, meeting the
Watchman's eyes for just long enough.

The knowing flared, searing.

"Wrong one," Kael said hoarsely. "Your man's already gone. Dockside.
Third tier."

The Watchman hesitated.

That hesitation was enough.

The boy scrambled to his feet and vanished into the crowd, swallowed
whole by the city.

Kael staggered back into the passage as the Watchman cursed and shoved
past, too angry to question why he'd listened.

Kael collapsed to his knees.

The backlash hit him all at once. His head felt like it was splitting
open, pain radiating down his neck and spine. He retched, bile burning
his throat, and pressed his forehead to the stone floor, shaking.

Tears leaked from his eyes without permission, blurring the world into
meaningless shapes.

Never again, he thought dimly, though he knew it was a lie.

When the pain finally eased enough for him to move, Kael dragged himself
upright and leaned heavily against the wall. His legs felt hollow,
unreliable. The knowing had retreated again, but it lingered like an
afterimage, reminding him of what it could do.

He had helped someone.

It hadn't felt like help.

Kael wiped his mouth on his sleeve and forced himself to stand. He
couldn't stay here. The Watch would come back eventually, if only to ask
questions.

He needed distance. Direction.

He looked down toward the lower levels, where the city grew darker and
the air heavier. Toward the places built to contain weight rather than
people. Toward the truth Gravewater had been hiding beneath iron and
water.

Kael's stomach clenched.

"I don't want this," he whispered to no one.

The city didn't answer.

He started walking anyway, each step carrying him farther from what
little safety he had left and closer to something he didn't yet
understand---but could no longer ignore.
