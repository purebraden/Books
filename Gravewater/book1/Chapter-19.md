# CHAPTER 19

Kael's ankle finally betrayed him on a bridge that had no business being
there.

It was narrow and old, slung between two buildings that leaned toward
each other like conspirators. The boards were slick with mist from the
channel below, and the railing was a suggestion more than a boundary.
Kael made it three steps before the joint in his leg folded with a wet,
grinding pain that stole the breath from his lungs.

He went down hard.

For a moment, all he could do was clutch the railing and gasp, teeth
bared, vision exploding into sparks. The bridge swayed under his weight.
Water slapped lazily against pylons

below, patient as ever.

Kael dragged himself upright on one knee, chest heaving. He tried to
stand.

His leg refused.

Behind him, the city closed in.

He could hear it now---boots on boards, shouts ricocheting down alleys,
the whistle's sharp punctuation as patrols corrected their angles. Not
close enough to touch him yet. Close enough to end this.

The knowing pressed at him, insistent, almost desperate. Look, it urged.
Choose. It offered routes---two alleys that might still be clear, a
ladder that would cost him a minute he didn't have, a cluster of bodies
ahead whose fear would hide him for seconds at best.

Seconds wouldn't be enough.

Kael slammed his fist into the railing in frustration and hissed through
his teeth. Pain flared bright and clean, anchoring him just enough to
think.

He could scream.

Draw civilians. Complicate the Watch's approach. Risk someone else
getting hurt.

He could crawl.

Slow. Obvious. Pitiful.

He could use the knowing and run blind on its guidance until it broke
him---or exposed him so thoroughly there'd be no denying what he was.

Kael swallowed blood and spat over the edge.

None of the choices were good.

A shadow fell across the boards at the far end of the bridge.

Kael looked up, heart hammering.

The man who stood there wasn't Watch. He wore a coat cut for weather and
work, not pursuit. No lantern. No insignia.

His hands were empty, held loose at his sides. He stood where the bridge
met solid ground and did not step onto it.

A smart position.

"Don't move," the man said, not loudly.

Kael laughed, breathless. "Wasn't planning to."

The man's gaze flicked to Kael's leg, then back to his face.
Calculating. "You're hurt."

Kael didn't bother denying it.

Behind the man, movement flickered---two figures holding back, not
entering the bridge. One of them Kael recognized: the dock man from
earlier, watching with narrowed eyes. The woman was there too, face set,
arms folded.

So they'd followed after all.

Kael felt something cold settle in his gut.

"You're bleeding," the man on the bridge said calmly. "If you fall into
the water from here, you won't get out."

Kael tightened his grip on the railing. "Then why are you here?"

"To stop this becoming messier than it needs to be," the man replied.

Kael snorted. "You brought friends."

"Everyone brings friends," the man said. "Yours just aren't nearby."

A whistle shrieked somewhere close---too close now. The sound bounced
off stone and wood, multiplying.

The man's eyes flicked toward the noise, then back to Kael. "You don't
have time."

Kael's jaw clenched. "I'm not going with them."

"I know," the man said.

He took one step onto the bridge.

Kael tensed instinctively, hand slipping on the damp railing.

The knowing surged, begging to be used---to tell him exactly how this
went wrong.

Kael forced it down.

"What do you want?" Kael demanded.

The man stopped where the boards creaked faintly under his weight. "I
want you not to make this worse."

Kael barked a laugh. "Bit late."

"Not as late as you think," the man said. "You've been running from
people who don't know what to do with you yet."

Kael's stomach tightened. "And you do?"

The man considered him for a heartbeat too long. "I know where you need
to be not right now."

The Watch rounded the corner at the far end of the street below,
lanterns bobbing, voices rising as they spotted the bridge. Someone
shouted Kael's name again, loud and sure.

The dock man behind the stranger shifted, impatience flashing across his
face. "This is taking too long."

The man on the bridge didn't look back. "Then leave."

The dock man hesitated---then, to Kael's surprise, stepped away with a
sharp curse. The woman followed, eyes lingering on Kael with open
calculation before she disappeared too.

They weren't done.

They were just letting someone else handle this.

The stranger on the bridge exhaled slowly. "See? Less messy already."

Kael stared at him, disbelief warring with exhaustion. "Who are you?"

"Someone with authority the Watch recognizes," the man said. "And with
enough patience to wait out dock opportunists."

The knowing throbbed hard at the word authority, tugging at Kael to open
himself, to verify, to see the threads of

obligation coiling around this man.

Kael didn't.

He was too tired to pay that price again.

"What happens if I say no?" Kael asked quietly.

The man's gaze softened---not kindly, but realistically. "Then the Watch
takes you. And after that, you won't see daylight for a long time. If
ever."

Kael closed his eyes briefly. The sounds of the city pressed in---the
boots, the shouts, the whistle cutting through it all.

When he opened them, he looked at the water below.

"I'm not agreeing to anything," Kael said.

"I didn't ask you to," the man replied. "This isn't a bargain."

The Watch reached the base of the bridge. Lantern light spilled upward,
painting the underside of the boards in harsh yellow.

"There he is!" someone shouted.

The man on the bridge stepped closer, careful, and extended a hand.

Kael stared at it.

Not a deal.

Not consent.

Just containment by circumstance.

Kael's leg screamed as he shifted, and the world tilted nauseatingly. He
knew, with the last clear part of his mind, that if he waited another
second, the choice would be taken from him entirely.

He took the man's hand.

Strong grip. Steady.

The man braced and helped Kael to his feet, taking most of his weight
without comment. The bridge swayed, then steadied.

"Easy," the man murmured. "I've got you."

Kael hated how much relief those words brought.

Below, the Watch shouted again, closer now.

The man turned Kael toward the solid ground at the end of the bridge.
"Head down. Let me talk."

Kael did as he was told---not because he trusted the man, but because
the alternative was chains.

They stepped off the bridge just as Watchmen surged into view at the far
end. Voices rose in protest, confusion flaring as the stranger spoke
sharply, producing something Kael couldn't see---a badge, a paper, an
order.

Arguments erupted. Orders contradicted each other.

Delay.

Kael leaned heavily against the man, vision dimming. His body was done.
The knowing pulsed faintly, exhausted, as if even it had run out of
warnings.

As they moved away from the bridge, into a waiting doorway Kael hadn't
noticed until now, he understood the shape of what had just happened:

He hadn't escaped.

He'd been taken out of circulation.

And wherever this man was taking him, it wasn't a place Kael could
simply leave.

The door closed behind them.
