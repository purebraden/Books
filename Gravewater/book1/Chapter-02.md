# CHAPTER 2

Mirek didn't miss meetings.

He was late often enough to make it a joke, but he never failed to show.
Even on bad days. Especially on bad days. If he said he would be
somewhere, he was there---complaining, grinning, carrying something he
shouldn't have, but present.

So when the sun climbed and Mirek didn't appear, Kael noticed.

He didn't say anything at first. He followed the route they'd agreed on,
took the long way around the lift, paused at the dry stairwell that
smelled faintly of old oil and salt. He waited the length of three
breaths. Then five. Then ten.

The pressure behind his eyes stirred---not sharp, not painful. Just
present.

Bram arrived next, silent as ever. He took one look at Kael and didn't
ask the obvious question.

"Tessa?" Bram murmured.

"On her way," Kael said.

They waited.

The city went on around them. A fisherman argued with a

supplier over a cracked crate. Somewhere above, a platform groaned under
a shift of weight. Water slid against stone, patient and unbothered.

Tessa came from the south route, moving faster than she needed to. She
stopped short when she saw their faces.

"He didn't double back," she said immediately. "I checked."

Kael nodded. "He didn't come through here either."

Silence settled between them, heavier than it should have been.

Bram spoke first. "Where was he last?"

Tessa answered without hesitation. "Lower channel. Old service cut near
the counting houses."

Kael felt the faint tightening in his chest before his mind caught up.
Mirek had mentioned that route the night before, offhand, like it meant
nothing.

"We check," Kael said.

They didn't argue.

The lower levels smelled different in the morning. Cooler. Staler. The
tide had gone out just enough to leave dark stains along the base of the
pylons, slick and uneven. Their boots left faint prints that filled with
water almost immediately.

As they descended, Kael noticed small things: a crate tipped on its side
that hadn't been there yesterday, a length of rope trailing into the
water, cut clean rather than frayed. He told himself not to assign
meaning yet.

The service cut lay where Tessa said it would, half-hidden behind a
collapsed barrier someone had never bothered to fix properly. Bram
slipped through first, then Kael, then Tessa.

The smell hit them all at once.

Tessa froze.

Kael knew before he saw.

Mirek lay half in the water, one arm twisted beneath him, the other
stretched out as if he'd tried to grab something that wasn't there. His
pack was gone. His boots were still on.

For a moment, none of them moved.

Then Tessa swore softly and knelt, fingers hovering just above Mirek's
shoulder before settling there, careful and precise even now.

"He's cold," she said.

Bram turned away, scanning the channel, eyes sharp and searching. Kael
crouched at the edge of the water, staring at the way Mirek's reflection
broke apart with every small movement of the tide.

Kael's hands shook as he reached into his own pocket without realizing
it, checking for something that wasn't there.

He catalogued details instead.

The way Mirek's head was angled wrong. The scrape marks on the stone
nearby, shallow but fresh. The water moving faster than it should have
in such a narrow cut.

Tessa stood abruptly. "We can't stay."

"Not yet," Kael said.

She looked at him, something hot and sharp flickering in her eyes. For a
heartbeat, Kael thought she might say something she couldn't take back.

Instead, she inhaled slowly and nodded. "Five minutes."

They worked without speaking.

Bram checked Mirek's pockets, then shook his head once. Tessa closed
Mirek's eyes. Kael adjusted his position just enough that the current
wouldn't pull him further under.

Kael felt a heavy, stupid guilt settle in his chest. He replayed the
previous day without meaning to---the moment Mirek had rushed, the way
Kael had corrected him, the casual confidence Mirek had shrugged into
place afterward.

If Kael had said more.

If he had insisted.

If he had gone with him.

None of it mattered. The city did not care about if.

A dockworker appeared at the far end of the channel, paused, then
quickly turned away.

Bram noticed. "Witness."

"Won't talk," Tessa said flatly.

"No," Kael agreed. "But he saw something."

Tessa straightened, jaw set. "We move him."

They lifted Mirek carefully, the weight of him wrong in Kael's arms. He
had always been heavier when alive, all motion and presence. Now he felt
like something the city had already started to reclaim.

They carried him to higher ground, to a place where water didn't reach
as easily. They laid him out, straightening his coat, arranging him as
best they could.

Kael stood back, suddenly unsure what to do with his hands.

The city did not pause. People passed at a distance, eyes sliding away.
Somewhere, someone laughed.

Tessa wiped her hands on her trousers and turned sharply. "This wasn't
an accident."

Bram met her gaze. "We don't know that yet."

Tessa laughed once, harsh. "You don't die like that by slipping."

Kael closed his eyes briefly. The pressure behind them swelled, then
settled again, like something testing a boundary he wasn't ready to
cross.

"We leave," Kael said quietly. "Now."

Tessa looked at him, searching his face. "And then?"

Kael had no answer that mattered.

They moved away from the channel, leaving Mirek behind with a marker
only Gravewater would recognize. As they climbed, Kael felt the absence
immediately---the timing off, the spacing wrong, the silence too wide
where Mirek's voice should have been.

The crew had lost a piece.

And the city did not care.
