# Epilogue — What Floats

Gravewater told the story it needed.

There had been an accident at the docks.

A shipment mishandled.

Bad lines.

Worse timing.

Men lost to the water, regrettable but not unusual.

The Watch issued statements.

Captains signed papers.

Coin changed hands in small, precise amounts.

The city moved on.

It always did.

The stair that never flooded was sealed with stone and mortar so fresh it still smelled wet.

The guard posts vanished.

The quiet ship’s name was struck from the harbor registry as if it had never existed.

Anyone who asked too many questions was reassigned work that kept them busy and tired.

Below the city, something pressed once against its boundaries.

Then did not.

Out on the water, far beyond where Gravewater’s excuses mattered, a ship cut through open sea under a sky that did not care who paid whom.

The crew learned quickly not to ask about the extra weight in the hold, or the quiet boy who slept where he fit and moved where he needed.

Kael stood on deck before dawn and watched the city shrink into shape and then into memory.

He did not feel victorious.

He did not feel hunted.

He felt elsewhere.

The sea rolled beneath him, vast and honest in its danger.

No alleys.

No walls.

No corners that remembered your mistakes longer than your name.

Behind him, Gravewater sank back into itself.

Ahead of him, the world waited.

And for the first time in his life, Kael did not count exits.

He counted horizons.
