# Chapter 28 — The Stair That Never Floods

Kael waited until the harbor forgot him.

That took longer than he liked.

The stair was where the man said it would be—cut into stone older than the docks themselves, half-hidden behind a break in the quay wall where repairs had stopped being worth the coin.

Water lapped close, but never quite reached the first step.

The stone stayed dry even at high tide.

That alone told him enough.

Kael didn’t go down immediately.

He watched the area for most of the afternoon, counting how often eyes passed over the break without lingering.

Once every few minutes.

Enough to notice if someone used it.

Not enough to remember afterward.

Good.

When he descended, he did it without hurry.

The stair smelled wrong.

Not rot, not mold—stale.

Like air that had been sealed and unsealed too many times without ever being refreshed.

The walls were damp but intact, chisel marks still sharp where erosion should have softened them.

Someone maintained this place.

At the bottom, the stair opened into a narrow passage that angled downward and inland, away from the water.

No doors.

No markings.

Just stone and shadow and the sense that the city above was pretending not to know this existed.

Kael paused, listening.

Nothing moved.

That was worse.

He advanced a few steps at a time, weight light, blade loose in his hand.

The knowing didn’t scream.

It tugged—soft, insistent—drawing him forward the way a current drew debris.

The passage widened into a chamber that had once been part of the foundations proper.

Old load-bearing arches had been reinforced with newer stone, careful work done by people who understood weight and consequence.

Crates lined the walls.

Not many.

Not stacked.

Placed.

Kael didn’t touch them.

He walked the perimeter first, counting exits.

Two visible.

One half-hidden behind a support pillar.

All narrow enough to be defended.

All dry.

Someone expected trouble down here.

In the center of the chamber stood a low platform of stone, newer than the rest, its surface etched with shallow grooves that didn’t form symbols so much as paths.

Not magic.

Not exactly.

Something that guided movement.

Kael felt the knowing tighten when he stepped closer.

Whatever had been stored here wasn’t cargo.

It had been contained.

He crouched, examining the grooves.

They weren’t worn evenly.

Pressure marks clustered at certain points, as if weight had shifted and resisted being moved where it was told. “Not a box,” Kael murmured.

Footsteps echoed faintly from the passage behind him.

Not one set.

Kael straightened slowly and stepped sideways, putting a pillar between himself and the sound.

He didn’t hide—he misaligned, letting sightlines slide where they wanted to go.

Voices followed. “…said he’d come alone.” “…doesn’t matter.

Door’s sealed.” “…careful with the floor.” Kael smiled faintly.

So this was part of the job.

They weren’t here to kill him.

Not yet.

They wanted him inside when something changed.

He let them enter the chamber first, watched boots test stone, watched hands gesture toward the platform with the careful respect reserved for things that bit back.

That was enough.

Kael moved—not toward them, not away.

Sideways, slipping into the half-hidden exit, body flattening against stone as lantern light swept past where he had been.

The men argued softly as he withdrew, unaware that the chamber no longer contained the person it was meant to.

Kael retreated just far enough to listen.

Whatever was stored here, it wasn’t stable.

It needed oversight.

Ritual checks.

Adjustments.

People who knew what they were doing.

And someone had decided to use him as a witness.

Or a trigger.

Kael backed up the stair without sound and melted into the harbor noise above, heart steady, mind racing.

The job wasn’t to steal.

It wasn’t even to confirm.

It was to be present when something went wrong.

And now he knew that.

He disappeared into the docks before the men below finished arguing, already planning how to return on his own terms.

Because whatever they were hiding under the city— —it wasn’t finished with him yet.
