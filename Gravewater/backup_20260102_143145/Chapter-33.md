# Chapter 33 — When the City Shows Its Hand

Containment failed quietly.

That was the mistake.

It happened just after midnight, when the harbor was loud enough to hide a scream and busy enough to lose a body.

A tremor ran through the docks—not an earthquake, not a wave.

Something local.

Intentional.

Kael felt it through stone before he saw it.

The prepared ship lurched against its moorings as if something inside had shifted its weight and then decided it disliked the answer.

Lines snapped tight.

Men shouted—not in panic, but in correction.

Orders were barked fast, clipped, rehearsed.

Too rehearsed.

Kael was already moving when the first death hit the water.

A dockhand went over the side without a sound, pulled hard enough that his boots came off before his body vanished beneath the surface.

The water closed over him without splash, as if it had been waiting.

That did it.

Shouts turned sharp.

Lanterns flared.

The harbor’s noise spiked and then fractured as people realized this wasn’t an accident you could explain later.

Kael didn’t run toward the ship.

He ran across the problem.

He cut through the outer ring of chaos, slipping past men rushing the wrong way, bodies converging where they thought the danger was.

He watched the people who didn’t panic—the ones who repositioned, who watched rooftops instead of water.

They were looking for him.

There was no more pretending.

A signal whistle cut through the air.

Another answered.

Watch—not officially, not openly, but close enough that no one would question uniforms later.

Kael crossed into open ground and let them see him.

Just long enough.

The first man lunged.

Kael stepped sideways and put him where he shouldn’t have been—slid him five squares into a tangle of lines and shouting sailors who had no idea whose problem he’d just become.

Another closed.

Kael vanished from his focus, reappearing behind him long enough to curse and leave a shallow cut that took confidence, not life.

The harbor exploded into motion.

Someone screamed about monsters.

Someone else shouted about sabotage.

Orders contradicted orders.

The prepared ship strained again, hull groaning as if something inside it was counting beats and finding them insufficient.

Kael felt the knowing tighten—not pulling now, not warning.

Condemning.

This was the failure state they’d tried to move offshore.

And now it was happening here.

A man in a coat too fine for the docks locked eyes with Kael across the chaos.

Recognition snapped between them like a blade being drawn.

That was the villain’s reach.

Not the hand.

The attention.

Kael didn’t hesitate.

He broke hard toward the inner docks, away from the ship, away from water that had decided it wanted payment.

Blades flashed.

Someone shouted his description too loudly.

A Watch captain made the mistake of pointing instead of ordering.

Kael slipped through bodies, through light, through the narrow margin between certainty and reaction.

He didn’t try to disappear completely.

He wanted them chasing.

Because every eye on him was an eye not watching the ship.

The water boiled.

Not dramatically.

Not with tentacles or spectacle.

Just wrong—currents reversing, lines pulling taut in directions no tide justified.

Another man went over the side.

Then another.

Panic finally won.

Kael vaulted a low barrier and vanished into the alleys bordering the docks, boots pounding stone as the harbor behind him descended into chaos that would be blamed on anything except the truth.

They chased him hard now.

No more steering.

No more subtlety.

This was a kill order.

Kael cut left, then right, then nowhere they expected, letting pursuit overcommit before slipping through overlap and shadow.

He heard a man fall hard behind him.

Didn’t look back.

The city had chosen.

Not to save itself.

To erase him.

Kael ran until his lungs burned and his blood sang, then vanished upward into broken rooftops as shouts echoed below.

He didn’t stop until the harbor was distant noise and the chase fragmented into guesses.

From a high perch, he watched smoke rise where no fire should be and lanterns swarm where order had failed.

They would survive this.

They would lie about it.

And they would never forgive him.

Kael wiped blood from his mouth and smiled thinly.

Good.

That meant there was nothing left to lose.
